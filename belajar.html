1. The Beginning of Aksikontal

Welcome to the Aksikontal Learning Center. This course begins with simple questions and develops ideas step by step. No advanced mathematics is required to begin.

Aksikontal is a developing personal framework that explores possible mathematical relationships involving mass, gravity, motion, voltage, energy, and probability.

Important distinction: Aksikontal is not an established scientific theory. Its proposed ideas must be separated from established science and tested before they can be accepted as scientific explanations.

How do we begin?

  1. Ask a clear question about a natural phenomenon.
  2. Learn what established science already explains.
  3. Define the variables and their units.
  4. Build a mathematical hypothesis.
  5. Compare its predictions with observations or experiments.

2. Understanding Gravity First

Gravity is an interaction between objects with mass. Newton's law of universal gravitation describes the gravitational force between two masses.

F = G × (M × m) / r²

Here, F is gravitational force, G is the gravitational constant, M and m are the two masses, and r is the distance between their centers.

For an object at distance r from the center of a spherical body with mass M, the gravitational acceleration in the Newtonian model is:

g = G × M / r²
  • g: gravitational acceleration, measured in m/s².
  • G: gravitational constant, approximately 6.67430 × 10⁻¹¹ m³/(kg·s²).
  • M: mass of the central body, measured in kilograms.
  • r: distance from the body's center, measured in meters.
Established science: If the central mass stays constant, doubling the distance from its center makes the predicted gravitational acceleration one quarter as large. This is an inverse-square relationship.

4. Developing the Aksikontal-G Hypothesis

Aksikontal-G is a proposed exploration of mass and gravity. The first step is to understand the established Newtonian model before suggesting any additional term.

Step 1 — Establish a baseline

g = GM / r²

This equation provides a reference prediction. A new model should first reproduce known results under conditions where Newtonian gravity is accurate.

Step 2 — State a hypothesis clearly

One possible mathematical exercise is to investigate whether a small additional term could be tested against the baseline model. For example, a tentative expression could be written as:

gA = GM / r² + K / r³

In this illustrative expression, K is an unknown parameter. For the added term to have units of acceleration, K must have units of m⁴/s².

This is only a proposed model form. It is not a verified Aksikontal law, and there is currently no evidence presented here that an additional term or an influence from another universe exists. The form and parameter would need scientific justification and testing.

Step 3 — What would testing require?

  1. Define the hypothesis and the physical meaning of K.
  2. Derive predictions for specific conditions.
  3. Compare predictions with reliable measurements.
  4. Estimate uncertainty and account for known effects.
  5. Check whether the hypothesis predicts anything measurable beyond the established model.

5. The Scientific Method

A formula is a mathematical description. Writing an equation alone does not prove that nature follows it.

  • Observation: record what happens.
  • Question: identify what is not yet understood.
  • Hypothesis: propose a testable explanation.
  • Prediction: calculate what should happen if the hypothesis is useful.
  • Test: compare the prediction with observations.
  • Revision: change or reject the model when the evidence requires it.
